01 / 13 — Investment memorandum

Investment thesis

Layerlab occupies the high-value middle ground between ad-hoc 3D print shops and tooling-heavy injection molding: rapid iterations, low minimum orders, consistent quality and scalable SKU economics.

Structural advantage

Low MOQ × fast iteration

Digitally controlled additive manufacturing allows small, custom batches without expensive production molds.

Execution thesis

One factory. Three distribution channels.

Shared printers and QA systems create cross-category operating leverage as product-market fit is proven.

02 / 13 — Investment memorandum

Market white space

Three demand pools share one manufacturing backbone. Motorcycle aftermarket provides repeat B2B demand; fandom merchandise drives high-frequency social discovery; padel creates premium positioning and customization opportunities.

12 / 13 — Investment memorandum

Risk register

Operating risks

Equipment downtime, failed prints, resin safety, electricity and raw-material availability.

Commercial risks

Unvalidated SKU demand, imitation, channel concentration and pricing pressure.

Each order card stores ID, SKU, variant, quantity, material, printer, deadline and status. WIP limit: eight simultaneously active printer jobs. A failed print creates a tracked reprint ticket.

Factory layout, inventory & shipping SOP

Two-bin material Kanban

One active supply bin plus one reserve bin per priority material/color. Opening the reserve triggers replenishment, sized to measured consumption and supplier lead time.

Stock-first scheduling

An order for 10 mounts with 3 approved finished units in stock schedules only 7 new prints. Approved stock and in-progress inventory are tracked separately.

Dispatch shelves: Ready to Pack / Packed Awaiting Pickup / Exceptions & Returns. Never mix uninspected output with approved inventory.
